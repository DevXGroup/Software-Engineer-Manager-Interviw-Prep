# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Working software engineers and tech leads preparing for Engineering Manager or SDM interviews at large tech companies. They use this on a laptop, usually in the evening after a full workday, in sessions of 20 to 60 minutes. The job to be done: find the topic they are weakest on, read or drill it properly, and know how much of the material they have actually covered.

## Product Purpose

A free, open-source study tool that turns scattered EM interview advice into one ordered curriculum: an eight-week roadmap, six topic tracks, per-company breakdowns, quizzes, and progress that persists locally. Success is a user who opens it a second and third time and can name what to study next without thinking.

## Brand Personality

Composed, senior, specific. It reads like notes from someone who has run the loop from the other side of the table, not like a course landing page. No hype, no countdowns, no "unlock your potential". Confidence comes from the density and accuracy of the material, so the interface stays quiet and lets long-form content and code be read comfortably at night.

## Anti-references

- Purple-to-blue gradient SaaS marketing pages (what this app looked like before this pass).
- Bootcamp and course-seller aesthetics: badges, blurred blobs, pulsing dots, urgency.
- Identical icon-card grids where every topic looks equally important.
- Cream and sand "editorial" AI landing pages.
- Dark near-black plus neon mint developer-tool skins.

## Design Principles

1. **Rank, don't tile.** The interface always says what to do next. Equal-weight grids are a failure to decide.
2. **Reading is the primary task.** Prose columns are capped, contrast is high, code is legible at night.
3. **Progress is a shape, not a percentage.** Coverage should be visible at a glance, per track.
4. **One accent, used for action and current state only.** Color that is not carrying meaning is noise.
5. **Motion reports state changes.** No page-load choreography; a study tool should already be there.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at or above 4.5:1 in both themes, non-text UI boundaries at or above 3:1. Every interactive target at least 44px on touch. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes transforms and keeps opacity only. Meaning is never carried by hue alone: priority, difficulty, and quiz state all carry a label or icon as well.
